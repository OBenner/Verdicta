# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Verdicta is built around evidence, independent verification, and honest
reporting. The same standards apply to collaboration. We expect a respectful,
inclusive, and technically rigorous environment in which people can challenge
ideas without attacking one another.

Participation is welcome regardless of age, body, disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity or expression, experience, education,
socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or
sexual identity or orientation.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful and considerate in disagreement, review, and troubleshooting.
- Critique code, architecture, and evidence rather than people.
- Make feedback specific, actionable, and proportionate.
- State uncertainty honestly and report only checks actually executed.
- Welcome new contributors and explain project-specific assumptions.
- Respect privacy, confidentiality, licensing, and the project's independence.
- Accept correction and repair harm where reasonably possible.
- Keep security reports, credentials, private source code, and personal data
  out of public project spaces.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, threats, or sexualized attention.
- Insults, personal attacks, trolling, or sustained disruption of discussion.
- Publishing another person's private information without explicit permission.
- Sharing private reports, credentials, or confidential materials without
  authorization.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern or participates in a review.
- Deliberately falsifying test results, verification evidence, authorship, or
  the origin of project materials.
- Repeatedly ignoring project boundaries after a maintainer has explained them.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all Verdicta project spaces, including the
repository, issues, pull requests, reviews, advisories, releases, and other
project communication channels. It also applies when someone publicly
represents Verdicta or its maintainers in an official capacity.

## Reporting a concern

Use the existing private GitHub Advisory channel:

**[Open a private report](https://github.com/OBenner/Verdicta/security/advisories/new)**

Begin the advisory title with **`[Conduct]`** so maintainers can distinguish it
from a vulnerability report. The advisory is used only as a private
communication channel: **do not publish it** and do not request a CVE.

Include enough information for a fair review:

- what happened, when, and where;
- the people or project roles involved;
- relevant links or excerpts, with unnecessary personal data removed;
- whether the behavior is ongoing or requires urgent action;
- any immediate safety or privacy concern.

Do not report the same matter in public issues, pull requests, discussions, or
commit comments. Do not include credentials, unredacted EvidenceBundles, or
private source code. If sensitive evidence is necessary, describe it without
attaching it and wait for the maintainer to arrange an appropriate exchange.

A report about the repository owner cannot be handled independently through
this channel. An independent escalation contact has not yet been established;
this limitation is explicit and must not be represented as independent review.

## Confidentiality

Maintainers will limit report details to people who need them for review and
enforcement, where reasonably possible. Reporter identity and materials will
not be published intentionally. Absolute confidentiality, anonymity, or a
particular enforcement outcome cannot be guaranteed.

Do not publish the advisory or disclose another participant's report while a
review is in progress.

## Review and enforcement

Maintainers will review the report, preserve relevant evidence, request
clarification when needed, and consider the context and pattern of behavior.
Where available, a maintainer not involved in the incident will conduct or
review the decision.

Actions must be proportionate to the behavior and its impact. They may include:

- a request for correction or clarification;
- a private warning with expected changes in behavior;
- editing or removal of violating content;
- temporary limitation of participation or moderation privileges;
- permanent removal from project spaces;
- a report to GitHub or another relevant platform.

Enforcement is not a public vote. Public discussion of the reporter or the
reported person is not required, and maintainers will avoid unnecessary
exposure of either party.

## Appeals and follow-up

A participant may respond in the same private advisory with additional context
or a reasoned appeal. An uninvolved maintainer will review the appeal when one
is available. Follow-up communication should remain private unless all affected
parties agree otherwise or a public correction is necessary to repair harm.

## Maintainer responsibilities

Maintainers must apply this policy consistently, recuse themselves when their
impartiality is reasonably questioned, and avoid using enforcement to suppress
good-faith technical criticism. They are also responsible for clarifying the
boundary between a technical disagreement and unacceptable conduct.
